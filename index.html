IYPT Physics & Presentation Breakdown

1. Why Acceleration is Non-Constant

The gravity torque about the hinge is $\tau = -Mg \frac{L}{2} \cos(\theta)$. Since moment of inertia is $I = \frac{1}{3}ML^2$, angular acceleration is:

α(θ) = -(3g / 2L) · cos(θ)

Because $\theta$ decreases continuously during the fall, $\cos(\theta)$ increases from $\cos(64^\circ) \approx 0.438$ up to $\cos(0^\circ) = 1.0$. Thus $|\alpha|$ grows from $31.2\text{ rad/s}^2$ to $71.3\text{ rad/s}^2$. Constant acceleration formulas ($\theta = \theta_0 + \omega_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$) are invalid!
